How to Play

Build a historical MLB lineup that can win all 162 games. A non-linear engine grades each pick's run-creation value, adjusted for era and defensive position.

Fill the lineup card. You draft one player for each of the eight fielding positions — C, 1B, 2B, 3B, SS, LF, CF, RF — plus one DH / Util slot that any player can fill. Once a position is filled it locks; no two players share a slot.

The Slot Machine. Each round spins a random team + decade and shows that club's top 15 hitters from that era — ranked by wOBA, the best single-number measure of offensive value — each with their best individual season and the positions they actually played. Pick a hitter, then choose which open position to play them at; many qualify at more than one (a guy who logged time at both 1B and 3B can go to either, if it's open).

What the engine values. Following the Moneyball insight, it scores hitters mainly on the two pillars of run creation:

  • OBP — on-base percentage. Not making outs is the scarcest offensive skill, so it carries the most weight.
  • SLG — slugging, i.e. power per at-bat.

Defense counts, WAR-style. Like Wins Above Replacement, the engine applies a positional adjustment: the same bat is worth far more up the middle than at a corner. A catcher or shortstop who hits well is gold; a slugger stashed at 1B or DH gets no defensive credit. So a great-hitting backstop can be more valuable than a bigger bat at first base.

Era adjustment. A .400 OBP in the 1960s isn't a .400 OBP in the 2000s. Every rate is scored against its decade's benchmark, so a dead-ball on-base machine can outscore a steroid-era masher.

The win curve is non-linear. Each win past ~150 gets dramatically harder; a true 162-0 needs a superteam that rakes and is strong up the middle.

One team skip and one decade skip per game — spend them on dead reels.

Modes. Classic shows the box-score numbers. Diamond IQ hides them — draft from memory alone.

Data: the open Lahman / Chadwick Baseball Databank (MLB seasons through 2021). Lists ranked by best-season wOBA within each team-decade.
